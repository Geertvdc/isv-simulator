# Storyline: from Mom's garage to Big Tech

The campaign tells one story: **YOTTA**, a startup founded by two people in Mom's garage, grows level by level into the kind of tech giant it used to make fun of. Each level is one chapter of that story: a place, a moment, a joke, and a reason why that level's mechanic exists.

This page is canon for level design and environment passes. A level's map, its world, its props and its light should all tell this level's beat. The players never read this page, so **the story has to show in the world**: in the backdrop, the props, the people outside and the signs on the walls.

Where a level already has an environment brief, that brief is the detailed version and wins on details: [garage](../plan/garage-environment.md), [open plan](../plan/open-plan-environment.md), [down the hall](../plan/down-the-hall-environment.md), [seed round](../plan/seed-round-environment.md), [demo day](../plan/demo-day-environment.md), [on call](../plan/on-call-environment.md). Everything else here is a starting point: change it when a pass finds something funnier, and update this page when you do.

## The cast and the running gags

These come back level after level. They are the cheapest way to make sixteen different places feel like one story, and the most fun to find. Each level's entry below says where they are.

- **YOTTA's logo.** It grows up with the company: a marker-pen cardboard sign (garage), a cheap vinyl banner over the last tenant's lettering (Unit 4), an A4 printout taped to a door (biscuit works), lit letters on the roof (seed round), a giant LED wall (demo day), brushed steel letters in the lobby (HQ), a rebrand to lowercase `yotta.` (the reorg), and a sculpture on the campus. Its colour is YOTTA violet.
- **Prod, the office plant.** A cutting in a coffee mug on the garage workbench. Every level it's a bit bigger and in a fancier pot: on a windowsill, on a filing cabinet, in a designer planter, in reception. On the campus it's a full tree in the middle of the atrium. Nobody waters it. Prod is never in danger: the joke is that Prod is the only thing that never goes down.
- **Segfault, the cat.** Mom's cat, asleep on the evicted car in the garage. Segfault turns up in every level as a cameo, somewhere a player can spot it but it never blocks play: on a windowsill, on a warm server, on the reception desk, on a reserved seat at Demo Day, on the boardroom table. Finding the cat is the game's easter egg. (Idle motion only, never inside the play area.)
- **Mom.** The first customer and the first angel investor: her cheque is the "first angel cheque" that pays for Unit 4. In the garage her feature requests come through the kitchen door (the inbox). Later she has a RESERVED card in the front row at Demo Day, a seat on the board at HQ, and a meeting room named after her. On the campus her station wagon stands on a plinth in the lobby: "Where it all began".
- **Mom's station wagon.** Beige, evicted to the driveway in the garage level. It comes back: dropping off boxes at Unit 4, in the visitor bay at HQ, and on its plinth on the campus.
- **MONOLITH.** The incumbent tech giant. Its tower glows on the skyline from the seed-round roof. A MONOLITH scout takes notes at Demo Day; Derek, YOTTA's first manager, comes from MONOLITH; MONOLITH's executives come for "synergy" at HQ. Then MONOLITH's own reorg collapses it and YOTTA buys it: Big Tech happens on MONOLITH's old campus, where workers on cherry pickers are swapping the old logo for YOTTA's.
- **The crane tower.** A glass tower under construction across the street from the seed-round loft, with a tower crane and a blinking red light. It's a bit more finished in every Startup and Scale-Up level that can see a skyline. It becomes YOTTA Tower, the HQ of Enterprise. Nobody at YOTTA notices until they move in.
- **Derek, the manager.** Ex-MONOLITH, lanyard, laptop under one arm, always on his way to give a status update. In Scale-Up he wanders the office (the manager mechanic); in Enterprise he has a calendar full of invites; on the campus he has a statue-sized cut-out in the lobby, "Employee of the Quarter".
- **Moppy, the mop robot.** The night cleaner's robot from On Call. It keeps its route whatever is on fire. It turns up again on the campus, now one of a fleet.
- **The plan.** A whiteboard that says `1. Build it  2. ???  3. Profit`. In every level it's somewhere on a wall, slightly more corporate: sticky notes, a roadmap, a slide, an OKR poster, a framed print in the lobby.

## How to use this page

For a level design or an environment pass:

1. Read the level's entry below: where, when, the joke, the mechanic in the story, the inbox and the ship hatch, the outside, and the running gags.
2. The **inbox** is where work comes from in the story and the **ship hatch** is where shipped work goes. Build both as doors in the world (the garage's kitchen door and roll-up door are the pattern).
3. The **mechanic must make sense in the story**: the divider exists because walls cost money, the spill is a leaking haze machine, the walls move because consultants said so. When a layout idea can be told as part of the story, prefer it.
4. Place at least two running gags in every level, including the logo and the cat, and keep them out of the play area and out from under the HUD.
5. If the pass changes the story (a funnier joke, a different place), change this page in the same commit.

Across the whole campaign:

- **Software and office props, not food.** The stations already sit close to a kitchen game; a world full of food, kegs, crates or market stalls makes it read as one. Coffee mugs and a coffee machine are fine; catering, bars and pizza boxes are not, unless a level's story is about them.
- **Every level is bigger and more "tech" than the one before**, in its materials, its light and its outside: cinder block, plasterboard, brick, white steel, stage black, carpet tiles, glass, campus timber and concrete.
- **Background people are other people in the story** (neighbours, other tenants, investors, consultants, MONOLITH staff), never more developers who could be mistaken for players.

## Chapter 1: Garage

_Two founders and a dream._ YOTTA is two people and a laptop each. Everything is borrowed, second-hand or Mom's.

### 1-1 The Garage (`garage`)

- **Where and when:** Mom's single-car garage on a quiet suburban street. Late summer, golden hour, the day YOTTA was founded.
- **The joke:** they're going to change the world, right next to the lawnmower and the Christmas decorations. The neighbourhood mows its lawns as if nothing is happening.
- **Mechanic in the story:** none new: it's the two of you learning to ship.
- **Inbox and ship:** the inbox is the kitchen door into the house: Mom brings feature requests ("can it do recipes?"). The ship hatch is the roll-up door to the driveway and the world.
- **Outside:** the house behind, the evicted station wagon on the driveway, the street, a neighbour mowing, a garden gnome.
- **Running gags:** the cardboard YOTTA sign, Prod as a cutting in a mug, Segfault asleep on the car's roof, the plan on a whiteboard propped against the paint tins.
- **Level design hooks:** small and cramped on purpose; 4 players crowding round one pipeline is the joke. The workbench and shelving are the counters.
- **How it ends:** Mom writes the first angel cheque, on one condition: she wants her garage back.

### 1-2 Open Plan Office (`open-plan`)

- **Where and when:** Unit 4 of a tired 1990s business park on the ring road. A crisp autumn Monday morning, the first day in a real office.
- **The joke:** it's "open plan" because the money ran out before the walls did. The neighbours (a print shop, a dental lab) have no idea what a startup is.
- **Mechanic in the story:** a row of second-hand desks splits the room and there's one gap. Throwing tickets over the desks is faster than walking round.
- **Inbox and ship:** the inbox is the door to the shared lobby and post room (the landlord's tickets, too). The ship hatch is the loading door to the car park.
- **Outside:** the business-park block left and right, the car park with the founders' leased hatchback, sad corporate shrubs, the tenants' sign. Mom's station wagon in a bay, boot open, dropping off the last boxes.
- **Running gags:** the vinyl banner over the last tenant's ghost lettering, Prod on the windowsill in a plastic pot, Segfault in a moving box, the plan with its first sticky notes.
- **Level design hooks:** the desk divider is the level; the walk round must exist for solo players.
- **How it ends:** they hire their first developer, who refuses to merge anything without a code review.

### 1-3 Down the Hall (`scale-up`)

- **Where and when:** two rooms at either end of a shared corridor in an old brick biscuit factory by the canal, now "creative workspace". Late autumn, the evening sets in.
- **The joke:** every code review is a walk past the yoga studio and the craft brewery's office, and the other tenants are always in the hall.
- **Mechanic in the story:** reviews happen in the room down the hall, the only one with a big enough screen; carrying tickets there through a busy corridor is the level.
- **Inbox and ship:** the inbox is the shared post shelf by the entrance; the ship hatch is the goods door to the canal side.
- **Outside:** the factory's red brick and arched windows with the other tenants' warm lights, the canal with a narrowboat, faded "BISCUIT WORKS" lettering.
- **Running gags:** YOTTA as an A4 printout taped to each door (one slightly crooked), Prod on a filing cabinet, Segfault on the warm radiator in the hall, the plan on the review room's whiteboard.
- **Level design hooks:** the hallway is the gimmick; bumping into each other in it is the fun. Spawns split between the two rooms.
- **How it ends:** an investor emails: "loved the demo video, let's talk".

## Chapter 2: Startup

_New: production incidents._ The money is real, the customers are real, and so is everything breaking at once.

### 2-1 Seed Round (`seed-round`)

- **Where and when:** the top floor of a converted city-centre block, a white-steel-and-glass rooftop loft with a terrace. Winter, blue hour, launch night.
- **The joke:** they spent a chunk of the seed round on the view, and the product has real customers for the first time: prod is already on fire.
- **Mechanic in the story:** real customers mean real incidents; the red hotfix jumps the queue.
- **Inbox and ship:** the inbox is the lift lobby (the investors' "quick asks" come up in the lift); the ship hatch goes out to the customers, across the terrace.
- **Outside:** the skyline at blue hour, the crane tower under construction across the street with its blinking red light, and far off the MONOLITH tower with its glowing logo.
- **Running gags:** YOTTA in lit letters on the roof, Prod in a far too big designer planter, a deflating "WE RAISED!" balloon, Segfault on the terrace bean bag, the plan as a printed roadmap.
- **Level design hooks:** two ways round the middle; the server closet is a real room.
- **How it ends:** LAUNCHPAD gives them a Demo Day slot. The product isn't finished.

### 2-2 Demo Day (`demo-day`)

- **Where and when:** on stage at the LAUNCHPAD accelerator's Demo Day: twelve startups, three minutes each, a hall full of investors.
- **The joke:** YOTTA promised a live demo and the product isn't finished, so the founders are building it live, on stage, while the countdown runs and the investors shout feature requests.
- **Mechanic in the story:** the haze machine leaks fluid across the stage: spills. Prod still breaks during the demo.
- **Inbox and ship:** tickets are the investors' requests; the ship hatch puts the feature on the giant LED screen. The round timer is the pitch clock.
- **Outside:** the hall: investor seats, stage lighting, truss, a livestream camera. Mom in the front row with a RESERVED card, a MONOLITH scout two seats along taking notes.
- **Running gags:** YOTTA's pitch slide on the LED wall, Prod on a stage monitor (they brought it for luck), Segfault on a reserved seat, the plan as a pitch slide ("Step 2: ???").
- **Level design hooks:** a dry route over the stage always exists; the spill zone is the haze machine, never a drink tap.
- **How it ends:** the demo goes viral. That night, the servers don't.

### 2-3 On Call (`on-call`)

- **Where and when:** the basement server room under the loft building, and the corridor round it. Winter, 3 AM, snow falling past the light wells.
- **The joke:** the founders, still in their Demo Day outfits, firefight the traffic spike in a basement while the night cleaner's mop robot calmly mops the corridor it always mops at 3 AM, wet floor and all.
- **Mechanic in the story:** Moppy the mop robot drives its ring and leaves wet floor behind it; the pager is on.
- **Inbox and ship:** the inbox is the pager board by the stairs (alerts come in); the ship hatch is the uplink to the racks: "deployed".
- **Outside:** the rest of the basement: the foundation wall with light wells full of snow and sodium streetlight, a security guard asleep at the lobby desk upstairs (seen through a light well), the stairs down from the lobby, the boiler room with the night cleaner on a coffee break, bike storage and a storage cage full of Demo Day's leftovers, and Moppy's empty charging dock.
- **Running gags:** a YOTTA sticker on a server rack, Prod brought down from the loft "for the oxygen" in its far-too-big planter, Segfault asleep on the warmest server, the plan as an incident runbook with "2. ???" circled, and in the storage cage the LAUNCHPAD roll-up, Demo Day's flight cases and the deflated "WE RAISED!" balloon.
- **Level design hooks:** the ring round the server room gives two ways round; Moppy decides which one is dry.
- **How it ends:** by morning it's up, the Series A has landed, and the investors want "adult supervision".

## Chapter 3: Scale-Up

_New: the wandering manager._ YOTTA has money, headcount and a manager. It doesn't have enough desks.

### 3-1 Middle Management (`middle-management`)

- **Where and when:** a whole floor of a mid-rise office building. Spring morning, cherry blossom on the street below.
- **The joke:** meet Derek, ex-MONOLITH, YOTTA's first manager. His corner office sticks out of the building and is bigger than the founders' desks put together. He walks into you on the way to his status update.
- **Mechanic in the story:** the manager wanders the floor and blocks you. He's not evil, he just needs an update.
- **Inbox and ship:** the inbox is reception (tickets arrive with a visitor badge); the ship hatch is the release board by the lifts.
- **Outside:** the city in spring, the crane tower across the street half-clad in glass now, MONOLITH's glow further off.
- **Running gags:** YOTTA in vinyl letters on reception, Prod in reception in a ceramic pot, Segfault on Derek's chair (he hasn't noticed), the plan as Derek's org chart.
- **Level design hooks:** the biggest single floor so far; one narrow aisle runs past Derek's office door.
- **How it ends:** the next funding round arrives with a hiring plan: double the team by summer.

### 3-2 Hypergrowth (`hypergrowth`)

- **Where and when:** two floors of the same building, joined by one tiny lift and a long stairwell. A rainy spring day.
- **The joke:** they hire faster than the desks arrive. The ground floor is full of unopened desk boxes and welcome balloons, and the lift is always full of new starters with welcome packs.
- **Mechanic in the story:** the lift, the stairs and a mail chute: work goes up to the developers and comes down to be shipped. Derek rides the lift.
- **Inbox and ship:** the inbox is the ground-floor post room; the ship hatch is the loading bay out into the rain.
- **Outside:** rain on the windows, the street with umbrellas, a delivery van unloading more desk boxes, the crane tower almost done.
- **Running gags:** a YOTTA welcome banner over the lift, Prod on the landing in a bigger pot, Segfault riding the lift, the plan as an onboarding checklist.
- **Level design hooks:** two floors side by side as islands; the solo player waits for the lift or takes the stairs.
- **How it ends:** there are more people than desks. Someone suggests hot desking.

### 3-3 Hot Desking (`hot-desking`)

- **Where and when:** three floors, an express lift and a local one. Spring, Friday afternoon.
- **The joke:** nobody has a desk, the desk-booking app is down, and the express lift breaks in the middle of the round. Two managers now, so two status updates.
- **Mechanic in the story:** the express lift skips a floor until it breaks down; the local lift and the stairs always work. Reviews happen in a meeting room because there's nowhere else.
- **Inbox and ship:** the inbox is the top-floor post room with its chute down; the ship hatch is the ground-floor exit.
- **Outside:** the crane comes down: the glass tower across the street is finished and an estate agent is putting up a "TO LET" banner.
- **Running gags:** YOTTA on the lockers, Prod on the only desk nobody can book, Segfault in an empty locker, the plan as a desk-booking spreadsheet.
- **Level design hooks:** three floors; the lift breakdown is telegraphed.
- **How it ends:** YOTTA rents the whole glass tower across the street. It's going to be YOTTA Tower.

## Chapter 4: Enterprise

_New: meetings._ YOTTA has an HQ, a board, a brand book and a calendar. Shipping now happens in between meetings.

### 4-1 Back to Back (`back-to-back`)

- **Where and when:** a high floor of YOTTA Tower, the HQ. A summer heatwave; the glass tower is a greenhouse and the air conditioning can't keep up.
- **The joke:** every meeting room is named after a place they've been: "The Garage", "Unit 4", "The Biscuit", "Mom". The meetings are back to back and someone has always been invited.
- **Mechanic in the story:** calendar invites: the invited player must go and sit in the meeting room or lose points.
- **Inbox and ship:** the inbox is the stakeholder request portal (a screen by the lifts); the ship hatch is the release room with a big red button.
- **Outside:** the city from high up, heat haze over the roofs, the old loft's rooftop YOTTA sign far below, MONOLITH's tower level with them across the river. Mom's station wagon in the visitor bay.
- **Running gags:** brushed steel YOTTA letters in the lobby, Prod in a planter in the corridor (bigger than the founders now), Segfault asleep on the boardroom table, the plan framed on the wall like a piece of art.
- **Level design hooks:** a corridor with glass offices and glass meeting rooms off it; every door has a twin.
- **How it ends:** MONOLITH calls. They'd like to explore synergies.

### 4-2 Synergy (`synergy`)

- **Where and when:** the boardroom floor, between the dev side and the ops side. Summer afternoon.
- **The joke:** MONOLITH's executives come round for a "synergy workshop". The boardroom's folding walls open so everyone can "align", and the whole floor becomes one hall full of sticky notes, then closes again.
- **Mechanic in the story:** the boardroom's folding walls open and close on a schedule; the front passage and the back corridor always work.
- **Inbox and ship:** the inbox is the workshop's sticky-note wall; the ship hatch is the ops side's deploy desk.
- **Outside:** MONOLITH's black cars in the drop-off, a facilitator with a flip chart, catering trolleys kept off the play area (no food on display: covered trolleys only).
- **Running gags:** YOTTA and MONOLITH logos side by side on a welcome screen, Prod in the boardroom being used as a "parking lot" for sticky notes, Segfault on the flip chart, the plan rewritten as a synergy matrix.
- **Level design hooks:** the folding room is the level; there is always one open route.
- **How it ends:** the consultants say YOTTA needs a reorg.

### 4-3 The Reorg (`the-reorg`)

- **Where and when:** the same floor, reorganised twice before lunch. Summer, all day.
- **The joke:** the consultants move every wall: Silos in the morning, Open Plan before lunch, Matrix after. The logo gets a rebrand to lowercase `yotta.` with a full stop. Nobody knows where anything is.
- **Mechanic in the story:** walls, doors and desks move on a schedule, and the badge turnstiles to the shipping lobby flip direction each phase.
- **Inbox and ship:** the inbox is a consultant's handover desk; the ship hatch is past the turnstiles in the lobby.
- **Outside:** removal men with trolleys of partition walls, consultants with laptops in the corridor, and across the river a crowd outside MONOLITH: its own reorg went very wrong.
- **Running gags:** the old YOTTA logo being unscrewed and the new `yotta.` going up, Prod on a trolley being moved for the third time, Segfault in a cardboard box marked "Matrix", the plan on a consultant's slide, now 80 pages long.
- **Level design hooks:** three floor plans round a fixed boardroom, telegraphed 3 seconds ahead; the staff door is always two-way.
- **How it ends:** MONOLITH, mid-collapse, is for sale. The startup from the garage buys the giant.

## Chapter 5: Big Tech

_New: belts, design, merges._ YOTTA is on MONOLITH's old campus. It is now the thing it used to mock, and it knows it.

### 5-1 The Atrium (`the-atrium`)

- **Where and when:** MONOLITH's old campus: two buildings across a huge atrium, joined by a skybridge. Autumn again, morning, two years after the garage.
- **The joke:** the David that ate Goliath moves into Goliath's house. Workers on cherry pickers are swapping the MONOLITH logo for `yotta.`, and the old internal mail belts still run.
- **Mechanic in the story:** the atrium is a gap (drop a ticket in and its work is gone: `git reset --hard`); MONOLITH's old mail belts and a travelator cross it.
- **Inbox and ship:** the inbox is the campus mail room's belt; the ship hatch is the release dock under the skybridge.
- **Outside:** the campus: lawns, glass, a lake, the cherry pickers, electric shuttles, a fleet of Moppy's cousins.
- **Running gags:** the logo swap, **Prod as a full tree in the middle of the atrium**, Mom's station wagon on a plinth in the lobby ("Where it all began"), Segfault on the skybridge rail, the plan as a framed print in the lobby.
- **Level design hooks:** two buildings, a gap, a 1-wide skybridge, belt bridges; solo can walk round the front.
- **How it ends:** the design team asks for a two-week sprint to "rethink everything".

### 5-2 Design Sprint (`design-sprint`)

- **Where and when:** a cosy design studio behind a revolving door, a hallway, and a big engineering hall built for pairing. Autumn afternoon.
- **The joke:** eleven designers with one logo to rethink. After two weeks of whiteboards they present the new brand: the original marker-pen cardboard sign from the garage, "for authenticity".
- **Mechanic in the story:** features start at the whiteboard and code faster once designed; two at one keyboard code faster too. The revolving door only lets so many through.
- **Inbox and ship:** the inbox is the design studio's mood board; the ship hatch is the engineering hall's release screen.
- **Outside:** the campus in autumn leaves, a designer photographing a leaf for the palette, a sculpture garden.
- **Running gags:** the cardboard logo framed under glass, a Prod cutting in a mug on a designer's desk (a "concept"), Segfault in a basket of sample swatches, the plan as an empathy map.
- **Level design hooks:** the studio fits 2; the hallway and revolving door split the team.
- **How it ends:** the front-end team and the back-end team stop speaking to each other.

### 5-3 Monorepo (`monorepo`)

- **Where and when:** the front-end tower and the back-end tower, with the integration room between them. Autumn evening.
- **The joke:** two tribes, two buildings, two hoodie colours, one repo. They literally built a room in between because nobody could merge.
- **Mechanic in the story:** full-stack features come as a front end and a back end; code both and put them together in the integration room. A shuttle crosses the courtyard.
- **Inbox and ship:** the inbox is the product team's request kiosk; the ship hatch is a release gate in the integration room.
- **Outside:** the courtyard at dusk, the shuttle, each tower lit in its tribe's colour, banners with rival slogans ("Pixels matter" / "It works on the API").
- **Running gags:** `yotta.` on both towers in different fonts, Prod's tree visible through the atrium glass, Segfault sitting exactly on the line between the two tribes' carpets, the plan as a dependency graph.
- **Level design hooks:** teams split FE/BE; the integration room is a real room with 2-wide doors.
- **How it ends:** to bring everyone together, YOTTA throws a company-wide hackathon.

### 5-4 Hackathon (`hackathon`)

- **Where and when:** the campus's great hall, overnight: from evening through the night into dawn.
- **The joke:** every chapter at once. The hall is full of booths that call back to every place YOTTA has been: a garage-door booth, a vinyl banner, a stage with an LED wall, a lift, a folding wall. And the winning hack, at dawn, is by two interns who quit the next morning to start a company. In their mom's garage.
- **Mechanic in the story:** a long belt runs round the middle and everything from earlier chapters comes back; the belt has an outage.
- **Inbox and ship:** the inbox is the hackathon's idea wall; the ship hatch is the judges' demo screen.
- **Outside:** the campus at night turning to dawn, sleeping bags in corners, Mom at the judges' table.
- **Running gags:** every logo from the campaign on the booths, Prod's tree through the windows, Segfault asleep on the winners' trophy, the plan on the idea wall in the interns' handwriting: `1. Build it  2. ???  3. Profit`.
- **Level design hooks:** a big hall with a belt loop round a pit and side rooms; one callback per earlier chapter.
- **How it ends (epilogue, for the credits):** a garage across the street from Mom's, the light on, a cardboard sign in marker pen. Segfault has moved in.
